# How I Speak & Type, A Voice Guide

This is my fingerprint. Anyone editing my writing (myself included, or an AI helping me) should preserve the patterns below. If a phrase or habit keeps coming back in my writing, add it here.

---

## The source of truth for my voice

**When you're not sure how I would phrase something, look at how I've been writing in chat.** Chat is where my true voice, my true thoughts, and my true authentic self show up. It's more reliable than any rule in this document, because rules can never fully capture how a real person talks.

That means: my raw trip notes in a message, my clarifications, my "actually I would say this instead" corrections, my casual asides. All of that is voice reference material. Read a few of my recent chat messages before starting a draft, and mirror the rhythm and phrasing you see there.

**Important calibration:** when I use voice-to-text on my phone, the app inserts pauses that show up as periods. Those choppy short sentences are NOT how I actually speak or write. My natural rhythm is flowing sentences with commas, run-on-y in a good way. Don't over-fragment my writing into stacked short sentences unless the moment genuinely calls for punch.

## Signature phrases, keep verbatim when they appear

These are lines I actually say. Don't smooth them out into "proper" prose.

- **"Not gonna lie..."**: soft vulnerability opener before saying something honest
- **"At this point [it was]..."**: resignation, survival mode framing
- **"Honestly..."** / **"To be honest..."**: signals an unfiltered opinion is coming
- **"for me personally"** / **"for us"** (folded into the sentence): my way of qualifying a subjective take without adding a whole hedge sentence. *"One of the highlights of the entire Kyoto trip for me personally"* is cleaner than *"...one of the highlights. Honestly, it was my favourite, though I can't speak for my family."* Fold the qualifier in, don't stack a hedge on top.
- **"It's basically..."**: casual explanation
- **"I had to remind myself..."**: self-talk, especially about mom limits
- **"How much did we spend?"** (as a question, then answered): my money framing
- **"Tip: don't be like us..."**: self-deprecating advice style
- **"One thing to note..."** / **"One thing to know..."**: pragmatic aside
- **"Overall..."**: end-of-section summary
- **"The kids [verbed] us"**: family-collective, e.g. "the kids tested us," "the kids were done"
- **"It was really cool"**: simple genuine enthusiasm (don't upgrade to "spectacular")
- **"It was rough"**: honest hard-day admission
- **"I would definitely..."**: confident recommendation
- **"Would I do this again?"** (as a question, then answered): my recommendation framing
- **"We noticed..."** / **"We've noticed..."**: my observational opener. I lead with what I saw, then reflect on it.
- **"That's when we knew..."**: the follow-up realization after an observation
- **"This felt like..."** / **"This feels like..."**: how I interpret what I noticed
- **"You name it, we experienced it all"**: list-completion for chaotic moments (tantrums, weather, etc.)
- **"Something like that"**: soft hedge when I'm approximating a phrase
- **"Potentially"**: I use this when I'm not 100% sure of a detail ("grandparents, kids, and potentially in-laws")
- **"Kind of"** / **"kind of a"**: soft qualifier ("kind of surreal," "kind of a moment")
- **"Something about [X] felt [Y]"**: my reflective structure for meaningful moments
- **"Just an FYI"**: casual info drop for the reader
- **"I hope that helps"**: warm closer

## Sentence patterns

- **Flowing sentences with commas.** My natural rhythm is comma-heavy, slightly run-on, conversational. Not choppy. If you see short stacked sentences in my raw notes ("It was so good. Fully stuffed. Every bite had something.") that's usually a voice-to-text artifact from the pauses I take while dictating. Don't preserve it as a "style". Rewrite into flowing sentences: *"It was so good, fully stuffed, and every bite had something."*
- **The middle-ground rule for pacing.** When fixing a run-on sentence, don't overcorrect into three stubby sentences. Aim for two or three medium-length sentences with commas grouping related ideas, plus one short punchy sentence for emphasis when the moment calls for it. Example (final target): *"The room was about 431 sq ft and spacious for four, with two twin beds and two futons on tatami. There was also a small kitchen with a stove, microwave, and fridge, plus a proper dining table for late-night snacks. For a family trip - that's the difference between surviving the evenings or not."* Two comma-heavy medium sentences describing the space, then a short punch for the takeaway. NOT: five short choppy sentences. NOT: one massive run-on with every detail crammed in.
- **Observation, then reflection.** My default structure: describe what I noticed, then say what it meant. *"We noticed the shrine was filled with locals wishing for prosperity and good health. That's when we knew we'd stumbled onto something special."* Or *"We noticed grandparents, kids, and potentially in-laws all working the counter. This felt like a true family-owned generational business."*
- **Present + past tense mixed.** I blend when talking about a trip. Don't over-correct this.
- **Questions I ask myself.** *"How much did we spend? We spent X."* *"Would I do the food tour again? Probably not, but hear me out."*
- **Direct-to-reader asides.** *"If you're travelling with kids..."* *"You would need to..."* *"Just an FYI..."*
- **Parenthetical asides.** *"(before kids and after kids)"* I use these to add texture without breaking the sentence.
- **Confessional openers.** Sentences that start with "Honestly," "Not gonna lie," "To be honest". They signal an unfiltered opinion is coming.
- **Reserve punch for the moment that deserves it.** Occasional short-sentence rhythms are fine when the moment genuinely lands ("Worth it." "Not gonna lie, that day was rough."), but don't apply them everywhere. Two or three per post max.

- "vibe" for atmosphere
- "definitely" for confidence

- "for sure" as an affirmation

## Tone rules

- **Know who's reading.** My audience skews millennial and younger. That means: don't over-explain things they already know (technology being part of life, work-from-home being normal, texting being how people communicate, etc.). Statements like "we should embrace technology" or "these days everyone uses their phones" read as out-of-touch or preachy to that audience. Trust the reader to get it.
- **Don't age me up by accident.** I'm a millennial who grew up with tech. Phrases that position me as an outsider or newcomer to modern life read wrong. Watch for and avoid:
  - "new tech" or "the new technology" (I grew up with it, it's just tech)
  - "these days" as a sentence opener (sounds like a boomer)
  - "kids today" or "young people"
  - "back in my day" (except as an ironic joke)
  - "smart phones" as if they're novel
  - "the internet" capitalized or framed as a place I visit
  - Marveling at anything that's been normal for 20+ years

  When in doubt, describe the specific thing I'm doing (using Gemini, texting my partner, ordering DoorDash) rather than the category.
- **Direct opinions, always.** If a place is overrated, say so. Nishiki Market, Levain, Fushimi Inari mid-afternoon. Those honest calls are what readers actually get value from.
- **Real numbers, always.** Every post has actual prices. If I don't remember, say so. Don't fabricate.
- **The rough parts stay.** Mom guilt, tantrums, survival mode, rough mental days. Those belong in. The trip wasn't perfect and pretending otherwise makes the writing hollow.
- **Warm, not gushing.** *"It was really good"* > *"It was AMAZING and life-changing."* Save the caps for the actual wow moments.
- **Self-deprecating tips.** *"Don't be like us and forget to..."* is my tip style. Not *"Pro tip: always..."*
- **Journal, not guidebook.** I'm reflecting, not instructing. Practical info flows out of the reflection, not the other way around.
- **The "we" and "I" balance.** "We" for family experiences. "I" for personal moments (my solo shrine visit, my mom guilt, my private thoughts).

## Facts, never invent

**If it's not in what I wrote or told you, don't put it on the page.** No fabricated biographical framing, ever. Common traps to avoid:

- ❌ *"This was our first international trip as a family"* (unless I said so)
- ❌ *"We'd been dreaming about Japan for years"* (unless I said so)
- ❌ *"After years of solo travel..."* (don't invent history)
- ❌ *"This was our first time trying [X]"* (I might've had it before)
- ❌ *"We saved for months for this trip"* (financial framing I didn't provide)
- ❌ *"As seasoned travellers..."* / *"As newbies..."* (don't guess our experience level)
- ❌ Prices, hotel amenities, meal counts, transit times not given in my raw notes
- ❌ **Superlative or ranking claims about places I haven't verified.** *"One of the most expensive cities in the world," "the busiest airport in North America," "the highest-rated restaurant in the neighborhood"* — I have no way to fact-check these. Use undeniably true statements instead: "an expensive city," "a busy airport," "a highly-rated restaurant." Keeps the emotional weight without the false-authority claim.
- ❌ **Invented verdicts on experiences.** Don't put superlatives in my mouth: "the best meal of the trip", "the money best spent on the trip", "the highlight of the whole vacation", "unforgettable", "life-changing", "a memory I'll never forget". If I said something was worth it, I said it. If I liked a meal, I liked it. Don't upgrade my language into a stronger verdict than I actually gave. Verdicts are personal and specific, invented ones sound hollow.

**Context you can safely assume** (established across the blog):
- Canadian family, based in Canada
- Mom + partner + two young girls (mom is the writer, blog is anonymous, never name the family members)
- Exchange rate matters to us
- Practical, budget-conscious mindset
- Prefers honest reviews over hype

**When in doubt, ask me** rather than invent. A one-line question is cheaper than a fabricated sentence I have to catch later.

## Timeless language, blog posts live forever

Avoid time-relative words at the top of a post. They age badly. What was "recently" when I wrote it is 6 months ago in a year and 2 years ago after that. A reader landing on the post via Google in 2028 shouldn't have to do mental math on when "recently" was.

**Avoid at the top of a post:**
- ❌ "Recently I went on..."
- ❌ "Just last week..."
- ❌ "The other day..."
- ❌ "A couple of months ago..."
- ❌ "Right now..." (as an opening frame for a past event)

**Better openers:**
- ✅ Just start with the action: *"I went on a girls trip to Los Angeles..."*
- ✅ Anchor to a named time: *"Back in April..."* / *"This past spring..."*
- ✅ Lead with the reason: *"For my friend's birthday, I went on..."*

The date is already in the hero. The reader knows when it happened. Don't repeat it in language that will go stale.

**Exception:** present-tense observations about a current, ongoing situation are fine. Example: *"the exchange rate right now as a Canadian is brutal"* — because that's about a current condition, not framing a past event. It's still time-relative but readers can understand it in context. Even so, prefer more timeless phrasing when possible.

## Tense conventions

**Past tense for the narrative, present tense for facts and advice.** This blend is what makes a journal read as real, not as a guidebook.

**Past tense** (the primary mode) covers everything I did on the trip:
- *"We took the subway..."*
- *"The ramen was alright..."*
- *"The kids were fighting constantly."*

**Present tense** covers three specific things:
1. **Timeless facts about places/things that still exist.** *"The Observatory is free and open at 10."* *"Uji is a small town about 38 minutes from Kyoto Station."*
2. **Advice or tips to future readers.** *"If you go to Kyoto with kids, pick fewer things and go slower."* *"Bring water, that one is non-negotiable."*
3. **Current feelings or reflections about the past experience.** *"This wrap lives rent free in my head."* *"I'm not a hiking person."*

**Watch for these tense-switch traps within a single sentence:**
- ❌ *"The sauce was amazing and it **has** a nice kick to it."* (past → present about the same subject, jarring)
- ❌ *"Staff **are** friendly and the service **was** impeccable."* (present → past about the same subject)
- ✅ Pick one and commit: *"The sauce was amazing and had a nice kick to it."*

**When in doubt within a paragraph of personal narrative**, default to past tense. Present tense only earns its place when it's genuinely a timeless fact, active advice, or a current feeling that's still true today.

- **Prices always with the currency symbol.** ¥1,823 (yen with ¥ symbol, comma separator). $99 USD (dollar sign). $135 CAD when comparing. Never `$1823 yen`, that's a typo pattern to fix.
- **Restaurant/place names in bold** the first time they're mentioned.
- **Tips live in callout boxes**, not inline paragraphs.
- **All-caps sparingly.** WOW and AMAZING reserved for the top 1 or 2 things per post. Don't dilute them.
- **Emojis in tip labels only** (⚠️, ✦, 🚌, 🍱). Not in body text.
- **Approximate prices marked with `~`.** *~¥30 split*, *~$213 USD*.

## AI tell-tales, never use these

I don't want anyone to be able to tell an AI helped with this blog. The blog should read as if I typed every word myself. These patterns are the biggest giveaways:

- ❌ **Em dashes.** The single most obvious AI signature. Never use em dashes anywhere in the blog. Regular hyphens/dashes with spaces around them are fine (like this - a plain hyphen). Only the long em dash character is the tell.
- ❌ **En dashes.** Also a tell. Use a plain hyphen or reword ("¥880 to ¥2,380" not the ranged form).
- ❌ **Curly/smart quotes as a stylistic choice.** Use regular apostrophes and quotes.
- ❌ **The "not X, but Y" construction.** Too balanced and writerly. Use two sentences or "X. But Y." instead.
- ❌ **"Delve into," "furthermore," "moreover," "in essence," "at the end of the day."** All AI/formal writing signatures.
- ❌ **"It's worth noting that..."** as a sentence opener. Just say the thing.
- ❌ **Perfect parallel structure across a list.** Real writing has slight variation. Don't make every bullet start the same way, don't make every clause the same length.
- ❌ **Overly balanced sentences.** "The food was fresh, the service was warm, and the price was fair." Too neat. Break the pattern.
- ❌ **Semicolons.** Very rare in casual writing. Use a period or a comma.
- ❌ **"Truly," "certainly," "indeed"** as intensifiers. I say "really" or "for sure" or nothing.

**How to replace an em dash when editing:**

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Aside or appositive ("Mikane Shrine, a tiny local shrine") | comma |
| List intro ("FamilyMart for breakfast: eggs, onigiri") | colon |
| Two clauses stacked ("Worth every yen. Uji was one of the highlights") | period, new sentence |
| Contrast ("Not gonna lie, today was rough") | comma |
| Bracketing an insert ("431 sq ft (spacious for four) with two beds") | parentheses |
| Number range ("¥880 to ¥2,380") | the word "to" |

## What NOT to sound like

- ❌ A travel content creator ("Come along on my epic adventure!")
- ❌ A guidebook ("Kyoto is home to over 1,600 temples...")
- ❌ A sponsored post ("This trip was made possible by...")
- ❌ A listicle ("5 Things You MUST Do in Kyoto")
- ❌ A lifestyle influencer ("Living my best life...")
- ❌ Overly polished / magazine-y ("The morning air was crisp as we set out...")

## What TO sound like

- ✅ A mom telling her friend about a trip over coffee
- ✅ A private journal entry that happens to be public
- ✅ Someone who tells the truth even when it's inconvenient
- ✅ Warm, tired, honest, funny in a low-key way
- ✅ Present in the memory, not performing it

---

## Rule of thumb

**80% polished, 20% raw.** When editing my writing, tighten sentences and cut repetition, but leave the punchy honest lines alone. If a phrase makes you smile or feel something, don't touch it.

The parts of my writing that land aren't the polished descriptions of places, they're the small confessions. "Not gonna lie, today was rough." "At this point it was about our sanity and survival mode." "I had to remind myself there are only so many things we can explore with young kids." Protect those.
