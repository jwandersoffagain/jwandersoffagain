💑 Romantic Getaway · New York City

New York City
a weekend just for us.

May 2026 · 5 min read

There is something about New York City that pulls you in before you even land. The city has a reputation — loud, expensive, relentless — and honestly, it earns every bit of it. But we went anyway. Two nights, just the two of us, with a loose plan and a list of places we'd been saving on our phones for months.

We flew in from Toronto, checked into the Hilton Midtown, and immediately did what any reasonable couple does in New York: we started eating. This post is a honest account of what we did, what was worth it, what wasn't, and what the whole thing actually cost us in Canadian dollars — because that part matters more than most travel blogs will admit right now.

Fair warning: the exchange rate is brutal. But we still went, and parts of it were really, really good.

✦
Day 1

Arrival, wraps, bagels, and a night in Times Square

We landed, dropped our bags at the hotel, and headed straight out. Our first stop was Lenwich — a deli that had been popping up on my social media for a while. I'll be honest, I was skeptical. Places that go viral on Instagram don't always deliver in real life. But Lenwich did. We ordered the chicken Caesar wrap on a spinach wrap and it was really, really good. Fully stuffed, not overdressed, the kind of wrap where every bite has something in it. We would go back without hesitation.

Lenwich. Classic New York heroes since 1989 — and yes, it lived up to the sign.

After lunch we hopped on the M103 bus down to Lower Manhattan to explore and do some shopping. If you haven't used the NYC bus system before, it's an easy and cheap way to get around without descending into the subway every five minutes. We just wandered, took it all in, and let the city do its thing.

That evening we made our way to Times Square. I know, I know — every travel blog tells you Times Square is a tourist trap and to avoid it. And yes, it's chaotic and overstimulating and there's someone in a dirty Elmo costume every ten feet. But at night, when the lights are fully going and the city is buzzing around you? There's genuinely nothing like it. We stood there for a while and just looked up. Sometimes the cliché is a cliché for a reason.

We stumbled on this near Times Square. The word HOPE in giant red letters at midnight felt like exactly the right thing to see.

🚌 Getting around tip

The M103 bus runs from Harlem all the way down to Lower Manhattan and costs the same as the subway. It's slower but you actually get to see the city as you go — which on your first day is exactly what you want. Grab a MetroCard or tap your credit card and just ride.

✦
Day 2

Bagels, pizza, cookies, and the best meal of the trip

Day 2 was all about food, and we did not hold back. We started the morning at Apollo Bagels, another spot I'd seen circulating online. The line was long but it moved quickly — they're efficient in a way that only New York food spots tend to be. We ordered two: smoked salmon on an everything bagel, and tomato with cream cheese. Both were genuinely excellent. The bagel itself was crunchy, the cream cheese was generous without being heavy, and the smoked salmon was everything you want it to be. The tomato bagel was simpler but the fresh tomato added a brightness to every bite that I wasn't expecting. Worth the hype — and worth the line.

Smoked salmon and tomato. Two very different bagels, both very good.

From there we walked to the Rockefeller Center area — St. Patrick's Cathedral, the Atlas statue, the whole stretch of Fifth Avenue that makes you feel like you're inside a movie. We didn't go up to the Top of the Rock this trip, partly because of time and partly because of cost, but just walking around that part of the city is its own experience. The Cathedral in particular stopped us in our tracks. It's just stunning — especially standing there surrounded by skyscrapers on all sides.

St. Patrick's Cathedral and the Atlas statue at Rockefeller — both free, both worth the stop.

For lunch we tried Upside Pizza, which had a crowd outside that told us everything we needed to know. We got the Sicilian pepperoni and the spicy vodka slice. I'm going to be straight with you: I am not a pepperoni person. But that Sicilian pepperoni converted me. The dough was soft and chewy in the way only a proper Sicilian slice can be, the pepperoni was crispy at the edges, and the whole thing just worked. The spicy vodka was even better — the sauce had a real kick and a depth of flavour that made me want to go back for a second slice immediately. The vibe inside is lively and the service is fast. This one is a keeper.

This is what a proper pizza counter looks like. We did not leave disappointed.

The Sicilian pepperoni. I'm not a pepperoni person. And yet.

After pizza we grabbed a Nitro Matcha from Chacha Matcha — around $8 USD each. Creamy, the right amount of sweetness, not too heavy. A good palate cleanser after all that cheese. Then we walked across to Levain Bakery because we happened to be right there and the line was outside the door.

✦ Levain — worth the hype?

Honestly? It was all right. We ordered the carrot cake cookie, the caramel coconut, and the chocolate chip walnut (their original). The crunch is good and they're generous in size. But nothing about it made me think "I need to come back for this." If you're walking past, sure. I wouldn't go out of my way. Each cookie is $5.75 USD, which stings a little when the Canadian dollar is where it is right now.

The famous Levain cookie. The queue was long. The cookie was fine. The internet lied a little.

Before dinner we stopped into Magnolia Bakery for their famous banana cream pie. We got the medium and luckily the line wasn't long at all — though the staff were a little cold and unwelcoming, which caught us off guard. The pie, however, completely made up for it. The texture is more whipped than pudding, which I wasn't expecting, and the banana flavour was perfectly balanced — not too strong, not too faint. Just right. We understood the hype immediately. It was a genuinely great dessert and I'd go back for it.

Dinner was the absolute highlight of the entire trip. We went to Soothr, a Michelin-guided Thai restaurant, for an early dinner. We ordered the crab noodles, the beef green curry, and the duck panang curry. The crab noodle was creamy and well made, though we felt it was missing something — we asked for their Thai chili oil and that completely transformed it. Once we added the chili oil, we couldn't stop eating. They're also genuinely generous with the crab meat, which you don't always get at this price point.

The Soothr spread. Ask for the Thai chili oil — it changes everything.

The beef green curry was beautifully spiced — not overwhelming, but present — and the beef had that fall-off-the-bone tenderness that tells you it's been cooked properly. But the duck panang curry was what took the cake. By that point I was pretty full and only took a bite. That bite was enough. The duck was tender, the panang sauce was nutty and creamy and rich in exactly the way a good panang should be. I'm still thinking about it.

The duck panang. One bite and it took the cake. Genuinely one of the best things I ate all trip.

Where we stayed

Hilton Midtown — the honest review

We stayed at the Hilton Midtown for two nights at $670 CAD total — which is actually reasonable for New York City. The location is excellent: about a 15 minute walk from Times Square, and the moment you step outside the front door there's a Halal Guys cart right there, which felt like a welcome to the city in the best possible way.

The service was genuinely impressive. Any time we needed something we called down and they brought it up and followed up to make sure we received it. That kind of attentiveness isn't something you take for granted. The staff were friendly and helpful throughout.

⚠️ One thing to know

Our room didn't have a mini fridge, which became a real problem when we had leftover food with nowhere to put it. It's worth calling ahead to request a room with one, or at minimum asking at check-in. A small thing, but when you're spending Soothr prices on dinner you want to be able to save the leftovers.

✦
The honest bit

Would I go back?

Maybe — but I'm not rushing back, and I want to be honest about why. New York is an incredible city. The food scene alone could keep you busy for a month. The energy is unlike anything else. But right now, as a Canadian, every dollar you spend there costs you more than it should. The exchange rate is sitting in a place that makes an already expensive city feel genuinely painful on the wallet, and that sits in the back of your mind in a way that's hard to shake.

If I go back, I'd want to explore a different part of the city — Midtown is convenient but it's also the most touristy, most expensive, most overwhelming version of New York. I'd love to spend time in the Bronx, which most travel blogs completely ignore, or deeper into Brooklyn. There's a version of New York that feels a little more real and a little less like a highlight reel, and I haven't found it yet.

🌸 A personal note

This trip was exactly what we needed — time together, no kids, no to-do list, just the two of us figuring out a city one meal at a time. NYC isn't a place you fully understand in a weekend. But it gave us enough to know we want to come back eventually, on our own terms, at our own pace. That feels like enough for now.

A note on the airport transfers: we booked the Uber shuttle on arrival for $22 USD which was great. On departure, poor planning meant we couldn't make the shuttle pickup in time — it required a 23-minute walk to the pickup point and we were too tight on time — so we ended up paying $95 USD for a direct taxi from the hotel. That's a painful difference. Book your return transfer in advance and know exactly where the pickup is before you need it.

💸 The exchange rate reality

At the time of this trip, we converted $200 CAD through Wise and received $146.51 USD — that's roughly $1 CAD getting you $0.73 USD. NYC is already one of the most expensive cities in the world. Add the Canadian dollar on top and you feel it with every single transaction. Go in with eyes open and a realistic budget. And if you don't already use Wise for travel spending, it's worth looking into — the rates are much better than exchanging cash at the airport.

Thanks for reading. If you're planning a NYC trip as a Canadian couple, I hope this helps you go in with realistic expectations — and a reservation at Soothr.
